## Findings, in priority order

### R1 — Ring overload proof is incomplete (technical substantiation required)

fault_protection_design.py defines ring I2 as1.9 times the calculated maximum ring-leg current. That leg current is the network result for two13A point loads; it is not the conventional operating current of a protective device protecting that leg. Multiplying it by a fuse factor and comparing with1.45Iz is therefore not, by itself, a demonstrated conventional overload-protection proof. The code's overall pass flag overstates this specific result.

This finding does not establish that the6mm²/B32 ring design is unsafe: the report also invokes the standard ring-final arrangement and checksIz>=20A plus a load-distribution bound. The correction should explicitly justify applicability of the standard ring rule, load distribution and fused spurs, and distinguish that justification from the computed nominal-current scenario. Avoid presenting the synthetic ring I2 comparison as an independent normative pass.

### R2 — Lighting selection rationale is incomplete (design-quality gap)

Existing evidence is not absent: four manufacturer LDT files are retained, and side_room_lighting_basis.csv estimates LC-F at506.7lx and LC-G at522.2lx using UF0.6/MF0.8 and assumed areas. However, lighting_zone_sensitivity.csv and lighting_alternative_sensitivity.csv explicitly contain preliminary/legacy counts and unverified boundaries. These cannot establish the final92-luminaire design as a whole. The gap is consolidation and justification for the final room schedule, not a claim that no lighting calculations were ever performed.

The answer adopts500lx and92panels but expressly does not establish maintained illuminance, uniformity or glare compliance. Device count, wattage and grid alignment alone do not demonstrate adequate task lighting. A concise room-by-room lumen-method check with justified utilisation/maintenance factors, or a documented review of the existing photometric work, would make the lamp quantity/power decisions defensible. The original task does not expressly require certified DIALux output; this is a gap in supporting the design, not proof that a separate specialist-software deliverable was mandatory.

### R3 — Several protection passes are conditional specifications, not product checks

SourceZe/PFC and total-clearing-energy bounds are adopted inputs. The scripts test whether those inputs fit the cable/CPC limits. They do not establish that an arbitrary selected C63 or RCBO actually has the required curves/let-through/inrush behavior. The answer mostly discloses this correctly, but FINAL_ACCEPTANCE and the general all-checks-pass wording can be misread. Clearly distinguish calculated cable suitability under declared apparatus requirements from a verified manufacturer selection. Coursework may reasonably use a performance specification if stated and defensible; real procurement approval is not required by the original assignment.

### R4 —13A fused-point demand restriction must remain explicit

The48.909A maximum demand depends on all nine floorboxes, three socket points and FS01 being individually limited by the specified13A fused arrangement. Four socket ways in a floorbox do not mean four independent13A load allowances. The report states this, but an explicit FCU connection detail and point-capacity legend would make it easier to defend and prevent readers treating the floorboxes as ordinary unrestricted ring sockets. This is a conditional assumption, not an identified arithmetic error.

### R5 — Route/grouping assumptions need clearer physical interpretation

Different circuit banks are assigned different grouping factors, while several plan paths share coordinates. Coincident schematic routes do not prove circuits share one containment, nor prove their thermal separation. Add a clear containment/spacing note explaining how the assumed grouping is achieved. Do not claim the grouping calculation alone validates the physical installation.

### R6 — Acceptance records conflate different kinds of evidence

863native handles present and unchanged hashes prove saved objects/file integrity. They do not prove compliant room clearances, useful socket coverage, adequate lighting or correct cable grouping. Similarly, served rectangles defined by the design script show that the chosen envelopes meet area limits; they are not independently measured architectural room areas. Keep these evidence types distinct in the acceptance record.
